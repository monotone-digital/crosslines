## Windows `/windows`

### Hero

- **Title:** The wall opening decides the window.
- **Summary:** Every type is available across all three profile tiers. The type decides how it opens. The tier decides how it ages.

### Four window types

- **H2:** Four window types
- **Copy:** One opening often uses more than one type. We work it out room by room.

| # | Type | Movement | Opening | Best for | Copy | Limited by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Openable / casement | Side-hinged | Full sash | Bedrooms, living rooms and exposed elevations | The default and the best-sealing window we make. The sash presses into the frame so the seal compresses rather than slides. | It needs clear space to swing. On a narrow side passage or above a footpath, a slider may be the better answer. |
| 02 | Sliding, 2-track | Slides horizontally | Up to half | Compact rooms, passages and everyday openings | Two sashes on two tracks, each passing the other. Nothing swings into the room and half the opening can stand open. | A sliding sash brushes past its track, so it will never seal as tightly as a casement on an exposed or dusty elevation. |
| 03 | Sliding, 3-track | Three sliding planes | Up to two-thirds | Wide openings or a dedicated insect-mesh track | A wider system for more glass in motion, or for carrying an insect mesh on a track of its own. | The frame is deeper and there is more track to keep clean. |
| 04 | Fixed | No moving parts | None | Views, daylight and hard-to-reach glazing | Light and view with nothing to open or seal against. The tightest and most economical option per square foot. | No ventilation, so it is usually paired with an openable sash in the same run. |

### Glass (`#glass`)

- **Eyebrow:** Glass
- **H2:** One pane, two or three.
- **Copy:** The sealed cavity between panes slows heat because trapped air conducts it poorly. In Multan, that earns its money on a summer afternoon—and again on a main road, where the second pane cuts noise.
- **Thicknesses:** 10mm — Our standard · 8mm — For smaller openings · 12mm — For large spans or noise
- **Aside:** A bedroom on a main road and a store room off an internal courtyard are not the same problem. We specify the glass per opening.

### Finishes

- **H2:** Finishes
- **Copy:** White, woodgrain and black.

| Finish | Description |
| --- | --- |
| White | Clean and architectural |
| Woodgrain laminate | Warm, natural character |
| Black | Crisp, contemporary contrast |

> You choose from physical swatches, so the colour you pick is not dependent on lighting in somebody else’s photograph.

### The frame decides the rest

- **H2:** The frame decides the rest
- **Copy:** The part you cannot change later. Glass, hardware and seals all fit into the profile. Its grade, chambers, reinforcement and seals decide how the window performs long after fitting.
- **Aside:** Most of that does not show in a showroom. It shows in year eight, in a frame that still closes tightly against dust and a colour that has not moved.

## Doors `/doors`

### Hero

- **Title:** Built for the weight they carry.
- **Summary:** A door is heavy, moves many times a day and carries a lock. We specify the frame, hardware and glass around those three facts.

### Door types

- **H2:** Door types
- **Copy:** The right movement for the span. Three ways a door can open, each trading swing space, seal and sash weight differently. The width of the opening usually decides which one is sensible.

| # | Type | Movement | Copy | Consider |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Casement doors | Hinged movement | Single or double-leaf hinged doors with the tightest compression seal we offer. | They require swing space, and wind can catch an open leaf. |
| 02 | Sliding doors | Two or three tracks | Two- and three-track systems for patios and terraces, with no floor space lost. | The seal is lighter than a casement and wide sashes become heavier to move. |
| 03 | Lift & slide | Lift, roll, then seal | A large sash lifts clear of its seals before rolling, then drops back to compress them when closed. | Its deeper frame, hardware and structural needs should be planned before the opening is built. |

### Lift & slide feature

- **Eyebrow:** Lift & slide
- **H2:** Wide glass. A proper seal.
- **Copy:** Turn the handle and the sash lifts clear of its seal, rolling almost weightlessly. Turn it back and the sash drops to compress the seal like a hinged door.
- **Aside:** Usually the right answer for a large living space opening onto a lawn or terrace. It is worth discussing before the walls go up.
- **CTA:** Discuss the opening

### Hardware matters

- **H2:** Hardware matters
- **Copy:** Doors wear at the parts that move. Hinges drop, rollers flatten, handles work loose and multipoint locks stop lining up. That is why hardware must be specified to the weight of the leaf.
- **Parts listed:** Handles · Hinges · Rollers · Multipoint locks
- **Aside:** Hardware is covered for twelve months. After that, we still source and fit replacement parts for anything we installed.

### Glass for doors

- **Eyebrow:** Glass for doors
- **H2:** The largest pane in the room deserves more thought.
- **Copy:** Single, double and triple glazing is available across all door types. We use 10mm as standard, with 8mm and 12mm available where the opening, exposure or noise level calls for it.
- **Aside:** On a door, the case for heavier glazing is stronger: it is usually the pane people stand closest to and the largest area of glass facing outside.

### Page CTA

- **Title:** Bring us the span before the walls go up.
- **Copy:** For wide sliding and lift-and-slide openings, an early conversation makes the finished door better—and prevents expensive corrections later.

## Profiles & Systems `/systems`

### Hero

- **Title:** Know the profile. Know who stands behind it.
- **Summary:** Five profile manufacturers across three tiers, supported by a practical guide to what matters inside the frame.

### Buyer’s guide

- **H2:** Buyer’s guide
- **Copy:** What separates a good window from a cheap one is not brand language. These are the things worth comparing in writing.

| # | Topic | Copy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Profile class | EN 12608 grades profile wall thickness. Class A is thicker; Class B is lighter and less expensive to produce. |
| 02 | Chambers | Hollow compartments trap air and slow heat and sound. Most quality systems run five or more. |
| 03 | Seals | Two seals is standard. Three holds a closed sash measurably tighter against Pakistani dust. |
| 04 | Frame heat transfer | Published as Uf in W/m²K. Lower is better. Do not confuse it with the whole-window Uw figure. |
| 05 | Reinforcement | Large windows and doors need steel inside the profile to remain straight. It is invisible once assembled. |
| 06 | UV and colour | Stabiliser chemistry and profile grade determine how the frame behaves under years of intense sun. |

### Six questions worth asking

- **Eyebrow:** Take this to any supplier
- **H2:** Six questions worth asking.

1. Which profile class is it, A or B?
2. How many chambers, and how many seals?
3. What is the frame’s Uf figure?
4. Is the frame reinforced, and where?
5. Is there a written colour guarantee, and how long?
6. Who covers the installation if something fails?

### Profile manufacturers intro

- **Eyebrow:** Profile manufacturers
- **Aside:** The company behind a system matters: documentation, continuity and access to the right profile all begin there.
- **H2:** Five companies. Three tiers.
